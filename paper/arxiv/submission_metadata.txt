Title: Sensor Metadata and Axis-Aware Tokenization Shape Zero-Shot Reconstruction in OPM-MEG

Abstract:
Electrophysiological foundation models must accommodate changes in sensor hardware, including wearable optically pumped magnetometers (OPMs). We test how one pretrained model, BrainOmni, behaves when the recording hardware changes, using only input interventions and no weight updates. Relabelling radial OPM magnetometers as gradiometers raises zero-shot waveform reconstruction from 0.2501 to 0.3819 Pearson correlation in all 21 participants of a single-axis cohort, and moves the same way in a single-participant SQUID diagnostic. The effect is configuration-dependent: on a triaxial cohort, the monolithic tag contrast is +0.0002 (p=0.969). A factorial crossing tag, filtering and partition shows that axis grouping beats an exactly size-matched random partition averaged over ten seeds under both tags. With soft homogeneous field correction (Soft-HFC), the advantage is +0.1071 under MAG and +0.0745 under GRAD, each in 10/10 participants. The paired interaction is -0.0326: the grouping advantage is smaller under the GRAD tag. Reconstruction gains do not establish decoding gains. On tactile Braille classification, a classical decoder over six log band powers and four temporal window means per channel clears chance under Soft-HFC while the pretrained probe does not. At ten participants, the decoding results remain inconclusive. These findings motivate testing continuous sensor parameterization and axis-conditioned queries, with reconstruction and decoding evaluated separately against competitive baselines.
